# Improve an existing site without losing what works

Use when the user wants to improve an existing site or app, preserve a brand or its
messaging, or correct a redesign that feels less useful than the original. An explicit
rebrand or replacement can justify broader changes; establish what that request
changes rather than imposing preservation of every old decision. A small defined edit
needs only a check of the affected region, not a site-wide inventory.

## Establish the baseline and the real improvement

Inspect the existing experience before judging the candidate. Use the actual page,
saved content and observed interactions, not only a hero screenshot or an inventory
of files. Identify what the visitor needs to understand, trust, find and do. For a
service business, this may include service scope, project evidence, team context,
reviews, contact actions, inquiry fields, practical information and quick links.
These are examples of visitor needs, not a mandatory template for every website.

For a substantial revision, keep a compact project-local comparison: existing
component or message, its purpose and prominence, proposed treatment, and the reason
for any change. Check the whole requested journey, including the footer and final
action. Presence is insufficient: moving a prominent action several screens down,
flattening detailed service content into a generic category or hiding a useful field
can reduce usefulness even if a similarly named component still exists.

Keep strong content, recognizable brand traits and working interactions by default.
Do not interpret “stand out,” “premium,” “cinematic” or a high craft score as permission
to replace the palette, remove practical information, create unusual navigation or
rewrite the company's voice. Equally, preserving useful content does not require
flattening requested expression or removing successful motion. Infer the boundary
from the request and existing decisions; ask only when a consequential ambiguity
remains. Existing authorization still covers routine, reversible improvements.

## Make the largest useful change first

Choose the next change from the observed obstacle to the visitor's task. A buried
contact action, unreadable headline, missing trust evidence or broken mobile menu can
matter more than further decorative polish. Explain the expected benefit as a
hypothesis when no visitor evidence exists. Do not claim a conversion increase from
an agent's preference.

Prefer changes that keep the successful component while fixing its failure: readable
text treatment around a working photo rotation, clearer labels on familiar controls,
or aligned images without deleting project depth. Keep accepted interactive behavior
unless an observed problem, user direction or implementation constraint justifies a
change. When broader style choices remain open, verify a representative slice before
expanding. Stop optional cosmetic iterations once the agreed improvements pass.

## Preserve visual energy as well as utility

When expression or motion is part of the desired improvement, compare it with the
existing running experience. Readability feedback is not a request to flatten
personality. Inventory successful galleries, animated entrances, interactive imagery
and disclosures alongside forms and navigation; a static replacement can be a loss
rather than simplification. Check presence, purpose and perceived effect separately.
Use [art-direction.md](art-direction.md) and the relevant [motion checks](motion-quality.md)
to preserve compelling behavior within the destination's actual capabilities.

Inspect important image subjects at relevant widths and during motion as well as at
rest. A fixed-height crop or moving image inside a clipped wrapper can cut off a
person's head despite a technically correct layout. Preserve the intended focal
content; use natural proportions, a deliberate focal point or movement of an unclipped
wrapper where appropriate. Static screenshots alone do not establish motion quality.

## Treat existing language as part of the design

Reuse supplied copy as the baseline. Preserve the business's positioning, promises,
qualifications, service benefits and recognizable phrases as well as literal facts.
A layout request does not automatically authorize copywriting. Improve casing,
punctuation, repetition and reading structure where useful; make larger changes only
when the brief calls for them or evidence identifies a comprehension problem.

Compare meaning and emphasis, not just whether the same keywords survive. Replacing a
clear company promise with invented image captions or generic lifestyle language can
weaken the message. Distinguish what an image visibly shows from a supported claim
about the project. Preserve authentic testimonial wording and attribution; label
excerpts rather than presenting paraphrases as direct quotations. Existing business
claims are source content, not facts independently verified by a design review.

Use the selected semantic or brand owner and one final prose editor when writing is
needed. This reference does not replace their copy workflow or add a second editor.

## Review the actual improvement

Compare the baseline and candidate against the same visitor tasks, content scope and
relevant viewport/states. Record what was retained, improved, deliberately changed or
left unresolved. Explicitly account for prominence, understandable language and the
shortest useful path to the action, as well as appearance and accessibility.

If using a rubric, apply its anchors to evidence. Do not award full credit merely
because a feature exists, or let visual polish average away a missing requirement.
Keep a comparable scoring method; label any rubric change instead of treating scores
under different definitions as an improvement. If user feedback exposes a mistaken
assumption, withdraw the unsupported conclusion and reassess the affected criteria.
A subjective score remains a review judgment, not certification or measured outcomes.

Use the [completion gates](quality-gates.md), especially saved-runtime and native-editor
checks for CMS/page-builder work. Preserve useful interactions in the destination
implementation, not only in a prototype. Recheck the parts affected by a material
change, and retain valid evidence for unaffected behavior. If original detail pages
remain alongside revised pages, describe that mixed journey accurately instead of
claiming a uniform redesign.

# Deliver the requested art direction

Use when the user names a visual reference, asks for an expressive redesign, or rejects
the result's palette, energy or sameness. This is a focused decision check, not another
design lead. Small defined edits and an accepted design system remain direct.

## Resolve the actual skill, not just its name

Before selecting a lead or operating layer, identify its provider, exact skill path,
and relevant instructions. An original and a derivative can both be called
`frontend-design`; the name does not establish equivalent behavior. Report the chosen
variant and any substitution. Do not silently label an opinionated derivative as the
original or stack both to average their preferences.

Separate user requirements and accessibility constraints from aesthetic heuristics.
Rules about avoiding particular hues, dark themes, gradients, serifs, rounded cards,
or animation describe possible defaults and tradeoffs; they are not universal bans.
The user's explicit direction takes priority. If a candidate's aesthetic rules oppose
that direction, select a compatible lead or apply a clearly scoped exception under
the user's instruction. Do not change third-party source or installed caches merely
to make one brief fit it. Correct the routing owner when selection or precedence is
the reusable problem.

## Separate purpose from appearance

Record two things independently:

- **Product to preserve:** audience, content, main tasks, objects, behavior and useful
  density, supported by the original app or brief.
- **Expression to deliver:** palette, contrast, type character, material, composition,
  and interaction or motion traits selected by the user or inferred from references.

Preserving an existing app's intention does not automatically preserve its old palette
or layout. A content/editorial audience requires readable articles and navigation; it
does not require cream paper, serif headlines, muted colors, a broadsheet or static
pages. When expressive exploration is part of the request, use one compatible lead
with Read and Experience concerns. Keep the content usable while delivering the
chosen expression. Conversely, an attractive reference does not authorize replacing
the original product with an unrelated scene or a narrower toy.

## Choose a palette positively

Build a compact palette from the brief, brand or selected reference. Name the base,
text, dominant color, accents and semantic colors, with their roles. Distinguish
observed reference colors from approximate or proposed values. Avoid choosing the
palette solely by excluding colors a generic anti-slop rule dislikes.

Inspect the dominant surfaces together. Repeated cream, tan, sage, clay, brown,
low-chroma neutrals or a dark glowing treatment can each become an unexamined house
style. If several concepts share them, check whether a shared brand was actually
requested. Do not carry an assessment document's theme into every product it links to
unless that is intentional. Saturation, darkness or color family alone neither proves
nor disproves quality. Never turn one rejected palette into a global prohibition or
claim a model-wide bias from a small set of outputs.

When taste is unresolved, show one representative slice or a small comparison with
the same content, then use the design-steering guidance. Once the user has chosen,
implement that direction without another approval round.

## Turn a reference into visible behavior

Extract two or three traits that matter to this brief. For each, keep a short mapping:
**observed reference trait → proposed implementation → evidence in the result**.
For an inspiration reference, translate the mechanism; do not clone its unrelated
subject, assets or whole layout. Keep the method in visual-resources.md for capture,
licensing and observed-versus-inferred motion.

For "dynamic," identify what responds to input, what moves or transforms, what content
it reveals or connects, how it interrupts, and its reduced-motion/touch alternative.
Camera travel, spatial navigation, a material response or a content transition can
provide character; a lone model viewer, indiscriminate entrance fades or changing only
colors may not satisfy the brief. The right mechanism depends on the user's intent.
Do not make text continuously move while someone is reading it.

## Make the composition coherent

Carry the chosen type, spacing, material and motion language through the requested
surface. Let section rhythm follow content; consistency does not require repeating
the hero or wrapping every region in the same card. Establish hierarchy and grouping
through type and proximity before adding containers or labels to repair confusion.

When a treatment feels arbitrary, name the element and its contribution to meaning,
state, action or the chosen expression. Try removing it: if clarity improves without
losing that contribution, leave it out instead of replacing it with another effect.
Preserve purposeful ornament and personality; this is a diagnosis, not a minimalism
rule or a ban on any technique.

## Verify purpose and expression separately

Check the representative page and its meaningful interactions against both records:

1. Can the user still complete the original task and access the intended content?
2. Do the requested color, contrast, composition and interaction traits appear in the
   actual result, including a meaningful start and finish state?
3. Has any generic rule or inherited theme displaced an explicit choice?

Use screenshots for appearance and exercise motion for behavior. A reference list,
successful build, accessibility pass or working filter does not answer the second
question. Record intentional differences and any unverified feel or timing. If a user
rejects the feel, diagnose selection and application before adding more skills or
asserting that the existing guidance has already handled it.

Retain settled taste in project notes; keep this shared rule generic. Do not store
private annotations or a user's project history here.

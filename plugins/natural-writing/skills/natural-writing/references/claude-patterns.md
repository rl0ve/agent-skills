# Claude-shaped writing patterns

Use this reference for Claude or assistant-generated prose. These patterns also appear in other models. They are tendencies, not fingerprints, and they change as models change.

## Dialogue residue

Claude often carries the manners of a helpful collaborator into prose that should stand alone.

- **Approval before substance:** “Great question,” “You're absolutely right,” or “That's exactly the distinction” becomes a ritual opener. Remove it unless the acknowledgment changes the relationship or argument.
- **Restate, then answer:** The first paragraph paraphrases the request. Lead with the requested artifact or conclusion.
- **Coaching theater:** “Three things improved and one thing I want to push on gently” stages collaboration instead of delivering judgment. Keep the useful judgment and its evidence.
- **Imagined reader management:** “You may be tempted to think…” or “To be clear, this doesn't mean…” introduces objections no reader raised. Preserve only real alternatives or objections the piece answers.
- **Offer loops:** “I can also…” and “Let me know if you'd like…” keep a chat turn open after a finished artifact. End where the artifact ends.

## Reasoning-shaped prose

Internal reasoning can leak into the final writing as visible scaffolding.

- **Taxonomy reflex:** A simple claim becomes three lenses, four layers, or a named framework. Keep a framework only when its categories change a decision, support comparison, or help retrieval.
- **Balanced-by-default stance:** Every claim receives a matching caveat or symmetrical counterpoint. Match qualification to the evidence, not to a desire to sound fair.
- **Self-answered questions:** “The catch? Timing.” or “What changed? Everything.” manufactures momentum. Use a declarative sentence unless the question serves the reader.
- **Correction-first argument:** The prose says what a point is not before saying what it is. State the positive claim first; retain contrast only when the rejected interpretation is plausible and important.
- **Local recap:** Each section ends with “The takeaway,” “Why this matters,” or a miniature conclusion. Integrate the consequence once and let sections end at different depths.

## Over-produced prose

- **Uniform helpfulness:** Every edge is rounded, every disagreement softened, and every paragraph resolves cleanly. Preserve warranted bluntness, ambiguity, and unresolved tension.
- **Significance at every scale:** A feature, sentence, and conclusion all announce their own importance. Let evidence carry emphasis.
- **Punctuation as cadence engine:** Dashes, colons, parentheticals, and fragments create rhythm even when the ideas do not require them. Rebuild the sentence rather than swapping punctuation mechanically.
- **Abstract personification:** Strategies “unlock,” systems “want,” and ideas “invite” without a clear actor or mechanism. Use a human or organizational actor when available.

## Creative and narrative prose

Apply these only to fiction, memoir, narrative essays, and brand storytelling.

- **Pace compression:** Every sentence strains to be vivid, revealing, or quotable. Allow plain connective sentences so important moments have contrast.
- **Device saturation:** Metaphor, personification, juxtaposition, and sensory detail appear because they sound literary. Keep each device only when it changes meaning, mood, or point of view.
- **Abstract-concrete image formula:** Phrases such as “bruised silence” or “a timestamp like a scar” repeat a clever surface pattern. Prefer one image anchored in this character and scene.
- **Explained subtext:** The narrator names every emotion, silence, and thematic connection. Remove explanations the action, detail, or dialogue already carries.
- **Psychological tidiness:** Characters understand themselves too quickly and behave consistently. Preserve believable contradiction, evasion, misdirection, and delayed recognition.
- **Convergent mood:** Ghosts, echoes, flickering light, melancholy technology, and soft apocalypse become default atmosphere. Choose details that belong to this setting and no other.

## Review method

1. Find clusters, not isolated phrases.
2. Decide whether the pattern harms this channel and reader.
3. Consolidate overlapping flags into the smallest useful diagnosis.
4. Fix architecture before vocabulary.
5. Re-read for lost claims, flattened voice, and overcorrection.
6. For changing model quirks, update examples and tests before expanding a blacklist.

# Design standard

## Reading order and separation

Use a white background, dark readable text, and one restrained blue accent. Use numbering, whitespace, and thin dividers to distinguish recommendations. Avoid equal-weight decorative card grids, dark presentation styling, excessive badges, or categories that need a color legend. Never encode status only by color.

Keep the issue heading, Problem/Impact/Proposed solution lines, and their evidence visually associated. Do not float a screenshot far from its explanation or strand its caption on another page. Prefer a natural top-to-bottom reading order. Keep a useful overview short; put supporting detail with the relevant issue rather than repeating it in a detached executive table.

## Typography starting points

Adapt to the medium, page size, audience, and density. These are starting ranges, not rules to impose on every output:

- Written brief: 11–12 pt body, 1.2–1.35 line spacing; 15–17 pt issue headings; 20–24 pt title.
- Problem/Impact/Proposed solution TLDR lines: body size or slightly larger, bold; retain enough contrast with the issue heading.
- Captions: about 9–10 pt for that document scale, dark gray, smaller than body text, with tight but readable leading and a small gap below the image.
- Web: start around 16–18 px body with 1.45–1.6 line height and a comfortable line length; maintain a clear heading hierarchy and responsive stacking.
- Presentation: size for the actual viewing distance. Reduce content instead of squeezing document-sized body text onto a slide.

One successful written-brief baseline is Arial 11.5 pt body with 14.5 pt leading, 12 pt bold TLDR lines, 16 pt issue headings, and a 20.5 pt title. Use 9 pt captions with about 10.5–11 pt leading. Adjust after rendering; never sacrifice legibility to preserve these exact values.

## Screenshots and images

Use actual current screenshots when discussing a visible issue. Prefer a relevant section crop that retains enough labels or surroundings to orient a new reader. Use the full viewport when overall layout or relative prominence is the issue; a crop cannot show prominence. Do not fabricate, redraw, or generatively replace evidence.

Place a thin visible border box around every image, including diagrams and mockups. A light gray-blue 0.6–1 pt border works for documents; choose a comparable subtle border on other media. Ensure the border remains visible in the exported PDF.

Annotate only when it reduces interpretation work: a small numbered callout, outline, or arrow aligned to the exact issue. Preserve underlying pixels. Prefer native shapes or overlays in the document or design format so annotations remain editable; keep the original screenshot available. Do not obscure important labels, change source text, or imply that proposed elements already exist.

For a guide to unfamiliar controls, use a uniformly enlarged crop or inset when needed,
numbered markers and an adjacent matching legend. Verify each meaning before labeling;
distinguish what a badge reports from what a control does. Follow the focused
[interface explanation](interface-explanation.md) method without imposing assessment
headings on a descriptive guide.

Use short subordinate captions immediately below images. Identify what the reader is seeing, the relevant issue, and an inline source link where available. Label before/after and proposed mockups unmistakably. Use semantic alt text in supported formats. If a screenshot cannot be obtained, state that limitation and use a clearly labeled schematic or text explanation, not simulated evidence.

## Tables and diagrams

Keep tables narrow and focused: compare a few alternatives or show specific current → proposed changes. Avoid long prose in cells, giant inventories, or a table that repeats the entire narrative. Repeat header rows across pages when unavoidable.

Use diagrams only when relationships, sequence, or current-to-future change would otherwise be hard to follow. Name nodes in ordinary language, label arrows if their meaning is not obvious, and explain unfamiliar terms. Separate current and proposed states explicitly. Prefer editable vector or native shapes when practical; verify labels at actual reading size.
